Choosing the Right Powder Coat Colour

RAL matching, gloss levels, UV stability, and how different environments demand specific formulations.

Guides / Choosing Powder Coat Colour

Selecting the right colour for your powder coated project involves more than aesthetics. The colour choice affects UV resistance, heat absorption, maintenance requirements, and the overall lifespan of the finish. This guide covers the key factors to consider when choosing a powder coat colour, from RAL standards to environmental performance requirements.

Understanding RAL Colour Standards

The RAL colour system is the industry standard for powder coat colour specification. RAL Classic includes 213 colours widely used in industrial and architectural applications across South Africa. Common choices include RAL 7035 Light Grey for industrial equipment, RAL 9005 Jet Black for architectural metalwork, and RAL 1023 Traffic Yellow for safety applications.

For projects requiring precise colour matching, RAL Design colours offer an extended range with hue, lightness, and chroma values for exact specification. Custom colour matching is also available, allowing you to match corporate brand colours or existing finishes. When requesting a custom match, provide a physical colour sample for spectrophotometer analysis to ensure accurate results.

Gloss Levels and Their Impact

Powder coat gloss is measured on a 60-degree scale and significantly affects the appearance and practicality of the finish. High gloss finishes (80+ GU) provide a reflective surface that is easy to clean but shows surface imperfections more readily. Semi-gloss (35-80 GU) offers a balance between reflectivity and practicality. Matte finishes (below 35 GU) hide surface imperfections and provide a contemporary appearance popular in architectural applications.

Consider the maintenance environment when selecting gloss level. High gloss surfaces in dusty industrial environments show contamination quickly, while matte finishes may be more practical for low-maintenance applications. The same colour can appear dramatically different at different gloss levels, so always request sample panels at your chosen gloss level before committing to a full production run.

UV Stability and Weather Resistance

South Africa's intense UV radiation presents a significant challenge for powder coat colour retention. Outdoor applications require formulations with high UV stability to prevent chalking, fading, and colour shift over time. Polyester-based powder coatings offer excellent UV resistance and are the preferred choice for outdoor architectural and industrial applications.

Darker colours absorb more UV radiation and heat, potentially affecting the substrate temperature and coating longevity. In high-UV environments, consider lighter colours or formulations specifically designed for South African conditions. Premium UV-stable formulations maintain colour integrity for 15 to 20 years in outdoor exposure, while standard formulations may show visible fading within 5 to 7 years.

Colour Selection by Application

Different applications have specific colour requirements. Industrial equipment and machinery often use grey or blue tones that resist showing dirt and grease. Architectural metalwork may follow corporate brand guidelines or complement building materials. Safety applications require high-visibility colours that meet regulatory standards. Transformer radiators and electrical equipment typically use light grey or white to minimise heat absorption and facilitate oil leak detection.

When selecting colours for multi-component projects, ensure all components are coated with powder from the same batch to prevent colour variation between parts. Batch-to-batch colour consistency is a hallmark of quality powder coating operations, and maintaining sample panels for future reference helps ensure long-term colour matching capability.
